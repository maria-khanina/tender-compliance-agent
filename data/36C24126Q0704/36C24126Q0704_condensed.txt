B.3 STATEMENT OF WORK – WATER TOWER INSPECTION & MAINTENANCE
B.3.1 INTRODUCTION
The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) Medical Center in Manchester, NH, requires a qualified contractor to perform a detailed inspection, comprehensive cleaning, preventative maintenance, and repair services on the facility’s elevated 200,000-gallon water storage tank located at 718 Smyth Road, Manchester, NH 03104.
This is a one-time service contract intended to ensure the water tower is fully inspected, cleaned, and repaired to maintain operational functionality, regulatory compliance, and structural integrity.

B.3.2 BACKGROUND
The Manchester VA Medical Center (MVAMC) operates an elevated steel water storage tank that must undergo routine inspections, maintenance, and repairs in accordance with Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), New Hampshire Department of Environmental Services (NHDES), Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), and National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) requirements.
The water tower has not received consistent inspection or maintenance due to the lack of a qualified vendor with proven expertise in water tower inspection, cleaning, and repair. MVAMC staff do not have the required technical expertise to perform these functions internally. Regular inspection and cleaning are required to ensure safety, potability, structural performance, and regulatory compliance.

E.2  52.212-2  EVALUATION—COMMERCIAL PRODUCTS AND COMMERCIAL SERVICES (OCT 2025) (DEVIATION)
  (a) Evaluation factors. The Government will award a contract resulting from this solicitation to the responsible Offeror whose offer conforming to the solicitation will be most advantageous to the Government, price and other factors considered. The following factors will be used to evaluate offers:
Factor 1 – Technical Capability
The Government will evaluate the offeror’s technical approach to performing the full water tower inspection, cleaning, maintenance, and repair services described in the SOW. The Contractor will:
• Provide documentation demonstrating understanding and application of all relevant AWWA, NHDES, OSHA, and EPA requirements.
• Provide a detailed inspection and cleaning methodology, including procedures, equipment, and verification steps.
• Provide technical specifications and procedures for ROV use in submerged surface inspections.
• Provide evidence of capability to identify structural defects, coating failures, corrosion, erosion, sanitation deficiencies, and contamination routes, supported by sample reports or prior work.
• Provide complete, technically sound work plans and operational procedures for reviewer evaluation.
Factor 2 – Experience & Past Performance
The Contractor will:
• Provide documentation of previous performance on potable water tower inspection, cleaning, or repair contracts.
• Provide evidence of performance in hospital or other high-risk environments.
• Provide records demonstrating contract quality, timeliness, customer satisfaction, safety compliance, and responsiveness.
• Provide three references and/or three CPARS entries within the past 3 years
Factor 3 – Key Personnel Qualifications
Contractor Personnel must:
• Provide proof of OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 certifications.
• Provide a copy of the NH Water System Operator License (certified Water Works Operator).
• Provide documentation confirming current standing with NHDES.
• Provide Confined Space Entry certification.
• Provide certifications for Fall Protection, Stairways/Ladders, and Lockout/Tagout.
• Provide evidence of coating integrity assessment training.
• Provide documentation of Healthcare Environmental Sanitation training.
• Provide evidence of demonstrated experience in structural water tower inspections.
Factor 4 – Safety & Compliance Plan
The Contractor will:
• Provide an OSHA-compliant Confined Space Entry plan.
• Provide equipment-specific Lockout/Tagout procedures.
• Provide a comprehensive fall protection plan.
• Provide procedures for SDS management.
• Provide documentation demonstrating compliance with hospital Infection Control requirements.
• Provide a defined hazard communication methodology.
• Provide evidence of safety controls for tools, ladders, cages, and access systems.

Factor 5 – Price
Evaluation will be the proposed total FFP price is reasonable for the defined scope.
Price is important but less significant than technical factors.
