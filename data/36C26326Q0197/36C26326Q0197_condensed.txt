B.2  STATEMENT OF WORK
Medical Gases and Gas Cylinders

1. SCOPE:   
  
1.1. The purpose of this requirement is to establish a 5 Year IDIQ Contract for Medical Cylinder 
Gases utilized at the VA medical facilities throughout Veterans Integrated Service Network 
(VISN) 23.  Specific locations are outlined in Attachment A, Facility Locations. The gases 
to be purchased include, but are not limited to, Compressed Medical Air USP, Oxygen 
USP, Carbon Dioxide USP, Nitrogen NF, Nitrous Oxide USP, Helium USP, and other 
specialty medical gas mixtures.  A full list of current known medical gas needs is outlined 
in Attachment C, Schedule of Items.  
1.1.1. The Government may request additional medical gasses or specialty mixtures at any 
time during the contract period of performance.  All contract terms and conditions shall 
remain the same and agreed upon rates for additional medical gasses will be 
documented through a bilateral modification to the contract.  
1.2. The contractor must comply with the delivery frequencies specified per delivery location as 
outlined in Attachment A. The Contractor shall have a facility and/or resources physically 
located in the geographic areas in which they will provide supplies.   
1.3. The contract items and quantities shown in Attachment B are estimates of each annual 
requirements.  There is no express or implied guarantee that these quantities will be 
purchased.  
1.4. The Government is anticipating awarding a fixed price indefinite delivery indefinite quantity 
(IDIQ) contract for five years. (5 Ordering Periods) 
1.5. The contractor will be provided with the name and contact information of the contracting 
officer representative (COR) within 20 days after award of the contract.  The COR will 
provide the contractor with the names and contact information of the designated points of 
contact (POC) for each ordering activity.   
1.5.1. The COR is responsible for the contractor’s adherence to the contract terms and 
conditions and is the primary liaison between the contracting officer (CO) and the 
contractor and ordering activity POCs.  If an issue cannot be resolved between the COR 
and contractor, the matter shall be referred to the CO for resolution. 
1.5.2. The ordering activity POCs are responsible for determining specific ordering details for 
their location’s medical gas requirements such as, but not limited to: ordering method, 
ordering quantities, types of gas, and delivery instructions.  If an issue cannot be 
resolved between the ordering activity POC and the contractor, the matter shall be 
referred to the COR and CO for resolution.

3.10. Lost or Damaged Contractor-Owned Cylinders: The contractor’s proposal shall include 
 the cylinder replacement cost for each item listed on Attachment C.

5.2.  The contractor shall submit an innovative plan with their proposal for tracking medical 
 gas cylinders being delivered to and from the ordering activity.  The cylinder tracking 
 plan shall be provided by the contractor with no additional cost to the Government.  The 
 primary purpose of this plan will be to provide a mechanism to allow the ordering activity 
 POCs on-demand access to view or extract data related to all cylinders, contractor-
 owned and Government-owned, that are moving in and out of their respective facility. 
5.2.1. The cylinder tracking plan shall include, but is not limited to: 
a) Method used for data entry and extraction (i.e. web-based portal, Microsoft Excel 
spreadsheet, SharePoint, email, etc.); 
b) Mechanism for data entry (i.e. barcode scanning, handwriting serial numbers, 
photos, etc.); 
c) How and when Government will be able to extract data; 
d) What type of data will be entered and stored; 
e) Limits on access to Government employees, if applicable; 
f) Any technology, equipment, software, etc. that requires connection to the 
Government network, if applicable; and 
g) Any other pertinent information related to the cylinder tracking plan 
5.2.2. If the contractor has an impending tracking system or one that is not yet fully operational 
but would like to submit as part of their proposal, the contractor shall include a timeline 
for implementation which must coincide with the phase-in timeline as described in 
paragraph 4.1.  Impending cylinder tracking systems that will not be fully operational by 
the conclusion of the contract transition shall not be submitted as part of the contractor’s 
proposal.  
5.3.  Although not required, if the contractor has a system for tracking medical gas cylinders 
 within an ordering activity’s facility after the cylinders have been delivered by the 
 contractor to the ordering activity, the contractor shall submit a tracking plan with their 
 proposal. 
5.3.1. The cylinder tracking plan shall include, but is not limited to, all items listed under 
paragraph 5.2.1.

6.1. The contractor shall submit in their proposal their available on-demand reporting 
 capabilities for the CO, COR and/or ordering activities in relation to usage statistics, cost 
 data, cylinder tracking and any other report types that may be beneficial for the 
 Government. Suggested reporting capabilities per type of report shall contain, but are 
 not limited to:  
6.1.1. Usage: 
a) Actual deliveries for any given set of dates; 
b) Average deliveries per week/month/year; 
c) Average deliveries per week/month/year per gas type; and 
d) Any other reports related to usage that may be beneficial for the ordering activity 
6.1.2. Cost: 
a) Actual billing data 
b) Average cost per delivery; 
c) Average cost per week/month/year per medical gas; and 
d) Any other reports related to cost that may be beneficial for the CO, COR and/or 
ordering activity 
6.1.3. Cylinder tracking: 
a) Perpetual delivery, pick-up and location data per cylinder; and 
b) Any other reports related to cylinder tracking that may be beneficial for the ordering 
activity 
6.2. The contractor’s reporting capabilities proposal shall include detailed information on how 
 the data is collected by the contractor (to ensure accuracy of data), the vehicle to which 
 the reports will be distributed (i.e. web portal, email, fax, in-person delivery, etc.) and 
 how the data will be viewed by the CO, COR and/or ordering activity (i.e. web portal, 
 Excel spreadsheet, Word document, hand-written on paper, etc.).

9.8.2. The contractor shall identify in their proposal any medical gases that can be classified as 
difficult to fill or likely to be placed on backorder by highlighting the item in Attachment C 
and by providing a statement justifying the contractor’s classification.

14. PROJECT MANAGEMENT:
14.1.  The contractor shall submit a Project Management Plan (PMP) with their proposal that 
 clearly demonstrates an understanding of the scope, schedule and service level 
 requirements of this contract.  At a minimum, the PMP shall include the following items: 
a) Communications and task matrix providing point-of-contact information and 
identifying and delineating the roles and responsibilities of key contractor personnel 
and resources; 
b) Individual responsible for providing the required contract deliverables and how 
the contractor will ensure the deliverables are delivered on time; 
c) A preliminary delivery, install, and user acceptance schedule constrained by the 
timeframes allowed with the contract with consideration of the interdependence of 
tasks in sufficient detail to identify and meet critical schedule deadlines; 
d) Estimate of how much time each critical task is expected to take and when each 
is scheduled to begin and end (time period in workdays or weeks); 
e) Outline of processes used to track and communicate delivery progress, 
installation, and document equipment acceptance; and 
f) Description of approach to identify the warning signs of potential project 
problems and their resolution process.
